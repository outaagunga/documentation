## 4. Interview Preparation (With AI — Clear & Practical)

### A. What You Practice (Exactly)

#### 1. Behavioral Questions (STAR — but tighter)

You are practicing answers to **predictable categories**, not random questions.

Common categories:

* Handling pressure or mistakes
* Conflict or difficult stakeholders
* Process improvement
* Deadlines / workload management
* Learning something new quickly

**Standard question examples:**

* “Tell me about a time you solved a problem.”
* “Describe a challenge you faced and how you handled it.”
* “Tell me about a mistake you made.”

👉 You should prepare **2–3 strong stories** that can be reused across many questions.

---

#### 2. Technical Explanations (Simple Language)

Practice explaining:

* What you do
* How a process works
* A tool you use
* A decision you made

**Test:**

> Can I explain this to a non-technical manager in 60 seconds?

If not, it’s not ready.

---

### B. How to Use AI (Concrete Prompts)

#### 1. Simulate the Interview

**Prompt:**

> “Act as a hiring manager interviewing for a **[role]**. Ask me behavioral and technical interview questions one at a time. After each answer, give feedback and ask a follow-up question.”

👉 This trains you for **pressure and follow-ups**, not just rehearsed answers.

---

#### 2. Pressure-Test Answers

After you answer, paste your response and ask:

**Prompt:**

> “Critique this answer as an interviewer. Identify weak points, missing impact, unclear areas, and suggest a sharper version.”

What AI checks:

* Is the problem clear?
* Is your role obvious?
* Is the impact specific?
* Did you avoid unnecessary detail?

---

#### 3. Reduce Rambling (This Is Key)

Take a long answer and use:

**Prompt:**

> “Reduce this answer to **60–90 seconds** without losing meaning. Remove filler, tighten structure, and keep it interview-ready.”

👉 You’re training **clarity under time pressure**.

---

### C. STAR Done Properly (Simple Structure)

Use this **compressed STAR**:

1. **Situation / Task** – 1–2 sentences
2. **Action** – 2–3 sentences (what *you* did)
3. **Result** – 1 sentence (impact or lesson)

**Bad sign:**
If Action is shorter than Situation → you’re narrating, not demonstrating.

---

### D. Technical Answers: Simple Formula

Use:

> **What it is → Why it matters → How I use it**

**Example:**

> “Excel is my main tracking tool. I use it because it allows real-time updates and audit-friendly records. I use formulas and structured tables to track stock movement and balances.”

👉 This works even for complex tools.

---

### F. How You Know You’re Ready

You’re interview-ready when:

* You answer without “uhm” and backtracking
* You finish answers naturally (not rushed, not long)
* Follow-up questions don’t throw you off

---

### 5. Identity Clarity (Personal Brand)

* Be clear on:

  * **Who I am professionally**
  * **What problems I solve**
  * **Where I add the most value**
* Ensure consistency across:

  * CV
  * LinkedIn
  * Interviews

---

### 6. Problem → Execution → Impact Rule

* Every experience must answer:

  * What problem existed?
  * What action did I take?
  * What was the outcome?

---

### 7. Final Quality Check

* **Concise** – No filler or repetition
* **Measurable** – numbers, scale, scope
* **Role-aligned** – relevant to *this* job, not all jobs

## 8. LinkedIn Execution (Clear, Practical, No Guesswork)

### A. Headline (Formula You Can Reuse)

**Format:**

> **Current / Target Role** + **Problem You Solve or Impact** + **Keywords Recruiters Search**

**Examples:**

* *Supply Chain Officer | Reducing Stock Loss & Improving Inventory Accuracy | ERP, Excel, Audits*
* *Data Analyst | Turning Raw Data into Business Decisions | Power BI, SQL, Excel*
* *Graduate Engineer | Site Supervision & Quality Control | AutoCAD, Project Reporting*

👉 Rule:
If a recruiter searches a **job title or tool**, your headline should help you appear.

---

### B. About Section (Compressed Story Framework)

Think **5 short paragraphs**, not an essay:

1. **Who you are**
   *Your role, level, or direction*
2. **What you work on**
   *Types of problems or responsibilities*
3. **How you create impact**
   *Results, improvements, efficiencies*
4. **Tools / skills you use**
   *Only relevant ones*
5. **Direction / openness**
   *What you’re targeting or open to*

**Example:**

> I’m an inventory and stores officer with experience managing stock across multiple sites.
>
> I focus on improving stock accuracy, reducing losses, and creating simple systems that work in real-world environments.
>
> In my recent role, I supported inventory audits, improved tracking processes, and helped ensure timely availability of materials.
>
> I work mainly with Excel, inventory records, reporting tools, and cross-team coordination.
>
> Currently open to roles in inventory, stores, and operations where accuracy and accountability matter.

👉 Rule:
Write like you’re explaining your work to a **smart non-expert**, not impressing peers.

---

### C. Experience Section (CV-Aligned, More Human)

**Don’t copy-paste your CV.**
Do this instead:

**Each role = 3–5 bullets**
Each bullet follows:

> **Action + Tool → Result**
> Worked in [organization type/team], responsible for [main tasks/tools], supporting [process, operations, reporting, or outcomes].

**Example:**

* Maintained daily stock records using Excel, improving stock visibility across departments
* Supported stock-taking and audit exercises, helping reduce discrepancies
* Issued materials to officers while maintaining accurate balances and documentation

👉 Difference from CV:

* CV = tight, formal, quantified
* LinkedIn = readable, narrative, still professional
